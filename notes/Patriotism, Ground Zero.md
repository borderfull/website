What can Thang village teach us about adaptability among the adrenaline-charged borders of India and Pakistan?

With the India-Pakistan discourse in India that has been providing an adrenaline rush to a billion-plus people for decades, border regions like Baltistan and villages like Thang are always under the spotlight in expectation of their patriotism and their unfettered love toward Bharat Mata (Mother India). 
Due to their dynamic identities across the changing northern borders of India, people in Baltistan, as in Kashmir, have always faced questions about their allegiance to the Indian state. There have been instances where arms and radio devices, among other things were suspected to be belonging to the Pakistani military, were found in the border villages, raising questions about villagers being involved in spying for the neighboring country. Rumors like this get circulated across India and tend to paint an anti-national picture of these border regions.

With the current rapid road network growth and increasing footfall of tourists in the region, the local people are again in the spotlight. Tourists who visit Thang expect to see 'Pakistan', and to learn about the stories and experiences of locals. In these times of increased attention, the local people in border villages have evolved. They have moved away from romanticizing their days when they were with the rest of their families and the rest of Baltistan, and could move easily with no borders to stop them. Instead, they are now showing the world how much Indian they are. The changing political environment in India has also majorly contributed to this evolution.

The people of Thang, through their stories of Pakistan, Indian flags on their houses, their hurling abuses of the neighboring country, and the stories of their sacrifices in helping the Indian army during different wars, show the tourists their limitless patriotism toward India. In doing that they show us how to be a model border citizen.
