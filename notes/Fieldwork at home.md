An ethnographer moves back home, bereft of fieldwork and landing straight into the aftermath of the loss of a loved one in the family, he must take on the initiative to understand his family. He talks to everyone, on every side, and 'analyzes' their pain and their stories about each other to find out what is actually going on.

It is common for me to pick up the pen and bring out a pocket diary as I begin noting down points from the ongoing conversations. Their eyes fell to see what was distracting them. With time, I learned to take notes after some emotionally charged conversations. Usually sitting opposite somebody from a borderland community in the Himalaya, these days my attention is on the family. A family that, as a result of my life abroad makes me feel coming down from the peripheries. I no more understand their quarrels, nor their cries. But here I am trying to listen to their arguments, unearthing what they are trying to make me understand, and that which they are not disclosing. 

The problem with the demised is that they can no longer confirm or deny anything. He can no longer even say - "no comments". However, comments are made in their name. Everyone has a version, everyone has a story to tell.  Trained as I may be for fieldwork, the struggle is real.
